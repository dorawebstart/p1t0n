02 • The problem

Two instruments, two different counts

VIIRS (375 m pixels) detects roughly 3–4× more hotspots per year than MODIS (1 km pixels) for the same fires — and up to 10–16× in some months.

Simply adding the two archives together creates a false jump in 2012, when VIIRS data begins — a change of instrument, not a change in burning.

04 • Method and math

How MODIS is put on the Suomi NPP scale

1 • Monthly scale factors

Ratio of sums on the overlap years

kAqua(m) = Σy SNPP(y, m) / Σy Aqua(y, m),   y = 2012 … 2020
kTerra(m) = Σy SNPP(y, m) / Σy Terra(y, m)

m is the calendar month. A ratio of sums is unbiased for totals: the scaled MODIS total over the training years equals the Suomi NPP total.

2 • Terra and Aqua separately

Different overpass times

Terra passes at ≈10:30 local time; Aqua and VIIRS at ≈13:30. Each MODIS satellite gets its own factor, then the two are combined, weighted by their raw counts:

H = w · kAqua · NAqua + (1 − w) · kTerra · NTerra
w = NAqua / (NAqua + NTerra)
3 • One series, three periods

Harmonized count H(y, m)

  • 2000–2011 scaled MODIS (formula above)
  • 2012 – Oct 2026 real Suomi NPP counts
  • next: after 2 Nov 2026 b · NOAA-20, b = Σ SNPP / Σ NOAA-20 over 2018–2025 = 1 / 1.033 (measured; not yet applied — the series ends Oct 2026)

Only one VIIRS satellite is used. Adding NOAA-20 on top of Suomi NPP would double the counts from 2018 — a new satellite, not more fire.

5 • Uncertainty

Small counts are noisy

Hotspot counts behave like Poisson counts, so the relative error is about

relative error ≈ 1 / √N

100 hotspots → ±10 %; 10 hotspots → ±32 %. Winter months with few fires are therefore much noisier than the summer and autumn peaks.

Winter months before 2012 have N in the single digits.

6 • Calendar statistics

Median, anomaly, expected range

M(m) = median of H(y, m) over all years
z = (H − M) / σm,   |z| > 2 is highlighted

The expected range is the 25th–75th percentile of past years for that month (shown when available). These are statistics of past years, not a weather forecast.

07 • How we built it

Data chain, code and credits

STEP 01

NASA FIRMS files

Yearly country files and global yearly archives (standard processing) for MODIS, Suomi NPP and NOAA-20.

STEP 02

0.25° monthly grid

Hotspots (vegetation fires, type 0) counted per 0.25° cell, month and satellite.

STEP 03

Harmonization

Monthly factors MODIS → Suomi NPP, validated on unseen years.

STEP 04

Calendar JSON

Monthly counts, medians and anomalies per region, saved as static files.

STEP 05

This static site

Reads the files directly in the browser; no server, no login.
