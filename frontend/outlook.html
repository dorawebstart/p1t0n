Where fires are likely in the next months

A seasonal outlook for the next 12 months, built from 25 years of harmonized NASA hotspots (2001–2025). For every 1° cell and month it gives the probability of an active fire month — at least 10 hotspots on the Suomi NPP scale. Recent years weigh more, so changes in the burning regime show up. We prove it on years the model never saw, including real fires in Ukraine in 2026.

How it works

Recency-weighted climatology

e(c,y,m) = 1 if H(c,y,m) ≥ 10 w(y) = 0.5^((2025 − y) / h) p(c,m) = (Σ w·e + a·p₀(m)) / (Σ w + a) E(c,m) = Σ w·H / Σ w

H — harmonized hotspots of cell c in year y, month m. h — half-life in years, a — strength of a prior that pulls rarely seen cells toward the month's global base rate p₀. Both are chosen by forecasting 2016–2020 from the years before; the test years 2021–2025 are never used for the choice.

The mathematics, step by step

How the outlook is computed and tested

1 · Event. For every 1° cell c, year y and calendar month m, take the harmonized count H(c,y,m) (MODIS translated to the Suomi NPP scale before 2012, real Suomi NPP after). An active fire month is e = 1 if H ≥ 10, otherwise 0. Ten hotspots in a ~10 000 km² cell is a fire season, not a single stray detection.
2 · Recent years weigh more. Year weights w(y) = 0.5(2025 − y)/h: a year h years older counts half as much. The outlook follows changes in the burning regime while still using all 25 years.
3 · Probability with a Bayesian prior. Treat e as a Bernoulli trial with unknown probability p and give p a Beta prior centred on the month's global base rate p₀(m) with strength a. With weighted observations the posterior mean is
p(c,m) = (Σ w·e + a·p₀(m)) / (Σ w + a)
Cells with a long record follow their own history; cells that rarely burned are pulled gently toward the base rate instead of an overconfident 0 or 1.
4 · Expected count. E(c,m) = Σ w·H / Σ w, the weighted mean number of hotspots. It is less reliable than p (years before 2012 had stronger spring burning, which lifts the mean), so the map shows p.
5 · Choosing h and a without peeking. Each candidate pair forecasts every year 2016–2020 from the years before it; the pair with the lowest Brier score wins. The test years 2021–2025 are never used for the choice.
6 · Scores. Brier score BS = mean (p − e)² (0 = perfect). Skill against a reference: BSS = 1 − BS / BSref; references are the plain 25-year average (all years equal) and “same as last year”. ROC AUC: the chance that a randomly chosen active cell-month got a higher p than an inactive one (0.5 = coin, 1 = perfect). Reliability: forecasts grouped by p, compared with how often fire really happened.
7 · A true forward test. The outlook built from data up to December 2025 is compared with real Suomi NPP hotspots in Ukraine, January–October 2026, data that did not exist when the model was fitted. Only cells fully inside Ukraine, so border cells are not undercounted.
Limits. A statistical, history-based outlook: it knows where and when fires usually happen and how that is changing, but it does not see weather (a dry spell or a wet month). Adding a fire-weather index is the next step. World data end in December 2025 (yearly NASA archives), so the outlook is built from 2001–2025.
